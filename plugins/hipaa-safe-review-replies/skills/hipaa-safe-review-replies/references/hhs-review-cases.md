# HHS cases about review replies

Checked: November 19, 2026, on hhs.gov. The Office for Civil Rights (OCR) at HHS enforces HIPAA. HHS has no separate guidance page on review replies. Its position comes from these enforcement actions. Older HHS press releases now redirect to an archive, so the links below go to the live HHS case summaries.

## The four review cases

| Practice | Year | Amount | What happened | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elite Dental Associates, Dallas, TX (general, implant and cosmetic dentistry) | 2019 | $10,000 settlement and a corrective action plan | Replied to patients' reviews on Yelp with their health information. | [HHS case summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/elite/index.html) |
| Dr. U. Phillip Igbinadolor, D.M.D. & Associates, NC | 2022 | $50,000 civil money penalty | Disclosed a patient's information online in reply to a negative review, then didn't respond to OCR. | [HHS case summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/upi/index.html) |
| New Vision Dental, CA | 2022 | $23,000 settlement and a corrective action plan | Replies on Yelp sometimes used patients' full names, where reviewers had used usernames, plus details of visits and insurance. The practice also had no policy for social media. | [HHS case summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/new-vision/index.html) |
| Manasa Health Center, NJ (psychiatry) | 2023 | $30,000 settlement and a corrective action plan | Disclosed four patients' information in replies to negative Google reviews. It had to notify each person. | [HHS case summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/manasa/index.html) |

The Yelp detail for Elite Dental comes from HHS's 2019 press release, which now redirects to an archive. The live HHS summary confirms the practice, the amount and the corrective action plan.

## A related case worth knowing
Cadia Healthcare Facilities, DE, 2025: $182,000 settlement. It posted 150 patients' names, photos and treatment as "success stories" on its website without written authorization. HHS said a valid written HIPAA authorization is generally needed before posting a patient's information in a testimonial or social media campaign. This matters for before-and-after photos. [HHS press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-hipaa-with-cadia-healthcare-facilities.html)

## What the cases have in common
- The practice was answering a negative review and added details to defend itself.
- Several had no written policy for social media or online replies, and the settlements required one.
- None of the four is a med spa. The rule applies the same way to any covered practice.
