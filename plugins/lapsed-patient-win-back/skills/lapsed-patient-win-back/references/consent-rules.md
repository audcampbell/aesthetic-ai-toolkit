# Consent and opt-out rules for win-back email and text

Checked: November 19, 2026, on official sources (hhs.gov, ecfr.gov, ftc.gov, fcc.gov, federalregister.gov). US federal rules only. States add their own rules on texting, privacy and medical advertising. This is a summary for planning, not legal advice.

## HIPAA: is a win-back message "marketing"?
Sources: [45 CFR 164.501](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.501), [45 CFR 164.508](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.508), [HHS marketing guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html)
- Under HIPAA, a message describing a health-related service the practice itself provides is generally not "marketing," as long as no third party pays the practice to send it.
- It becomes marketing that needs each patient's written authorization if a third party pays for it. For example, a manufacturer funding a promotion of its product. If unsure who paid, ask.
- Whether purely cosmetic services or retail skincare count as "health-related" isn't addressed on any official page found. Ask the practice's healthcare attorney.
- A practice can't sell or share its patient list with a third party without each patient's authorization.
- An email or text vendor that handles the patient list needs a business associate agreement (BAA) with the practice.
- Appointment reminders count as treatment and need no authorization. [HHS FAQ 286](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/286/are-appointment-reminders-allowed-under-hipaa-without-authorization/index.html)
- HHS's 2026 Privacy final rule (November 16, 2026) doesn't change the marketing rules.

## Email: CAN-SPAM
Source: [FTC CAN-SPAM Act compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- It applies to emails to past patients too.
- Accurate "From" name and a subject line that matches the email. No fake "Re:" or "Fwd:."
- Say clearly that it's a promotion.
- Include the practice's valid postal address.
- A clear way to opt out that works for at least 30 days after sending.
- Honor opt-outs within 10 business days. No fee, no extra questions, no more than a reply or one web page.
- The practice is responsible even if a vendor sends the email. Penalties run up to $53,088 per email.

## Text: TCPA
Sources: [47 CFR 64.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/section-64.1200), [FCC 24-24](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-24A1.pdf), [FCC 26-67](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-26-67A1.pdf), [Federal Register, Oct 26, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/26/2026-21821/enhancing-the-ability-of-consumers-to-control-the-robocalls)
- The FCC treats texts as calls.
- Marketing texts sent with an autodialer need **prior express written consent**. Whether a given texting platform counts as an autodialer is a legal question, and some states have stricter texting laws, so the safe practice is written consent for every marketing text. That means a signed agreement (an e-signature or online checkbox can count) that names the phone number, clearly agrees to marketing texts from the practice, and says consent is not a condition of purchase. Consent to appointment reminders is not consent to marketing.
- No marketing texts before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m., the patient's local time.
- A patient can opt out any reasonable way. "Stop," "quit," "end," "revoke," "opt out," "cancel" and "unsubscribe" all count. Honor it within 10 business days.
- One confirmation text after an opt-out is allowed, within 5 minutes, with no marketing in it.
- From November 25, 2026 (FCC 26-67): a sender can name one opt-out method, such as replying STOP, if every text says so clearly. All the standard words still count. An opt-out in reply to a marketing text stops all of the practice's marketing texts.
- Pending, not final: the FCC has proposed cutting the 10 business days to 7. Comments close November 25, 2026.

## What this means for the sequence
- Email: every email gets the practice's postal address and an unsubscribe link.
- Text: send only to patients with written consent to marketing texts. Every text names the practice and says "Reply STOP to opt out." Send between 9 a.m. and 8 p.m. local time to stay well inside the window.
- Anyone who opted out of either channel is removed from the segment before sending.
- No third-party-paid promotions without a HIPAA authorization from each patient.
- Use the practice's email or texting platform, under a BAA, to build and send. Patient lists never go into a general AI chat.
